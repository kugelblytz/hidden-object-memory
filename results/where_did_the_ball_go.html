Where did the ball go?

A small ball is hidden under one of three cups. Then, while nobody is looking, the camera moves around the table or one cup is moved. A robot should still know which cup hides the ball. This page shows, step by step, how five different systems try to answer that question, and how often each one succeeds.

Example trial 37: identical cups, camera moved 127° while unobserved.
The task

Two episodes and a gap in between

Every trial has the same structure. The difficult part is the gap: the system sees nothing while things change, and afterwards it has to connect what it remembers with what it sees now.

Episode 1 · observed

The ball is hidden

The camera watches a cup being lowered over the ball. Afterwards a short camera sweep records the scene from a few nearby angles.

→
Gap · not observed

Something changes

Nothing happens, or the camera moves 10–150° around the table, or one cup is moved 10–35 cm. The ball always stays inside its cup.

→
Episode 2 · question

Which numbered cup?

After a second short sweep, the system gets a final image with the cups numbered 1–3 and must name the cup that hides the ball.

Remembering "the ball is under the cup in the middle" is not enough: after the camera moves, the middle cup in the new image can be a different cup. The system has to work out which new cup is the old one. That linking step is what separates the systems below.

What this shows

Takeaways and limits

Memory beats re-watching

Every memory system beat a vision-language model that re-watched the key frames, by 18–28 points. Without any history the models are at or below chance.

Shared 3D frame is what matters

Only the joint VGGT pass stays at about 0.9 after large camera changes. Running VGGT separately and gluing the results with classic feature matching inherits the same weakness as the 2D memory.

Perception sets the ceiling

In about 8% of trials the shared perception loses track of the cup at the end of episode 1. Every system fails those trials, which caps all of them near 0.93.

Read with care

Simulation only, one task family, 30 trials per cell. The 2D memory does not yet reject bad table mappings, so it is weaker than it could be. The answer extraction for the language models was fixed after a first run cut answers off. The idea of one joint VGGT pass across time comes from earlier change-detection work (VGGT-CD); new here is its use for hidden-object memory.
